# Coaching and UX review

The intended product is a shared morning calisthenics class, without goal selection or skill deadlines. The sequence builds broad strength and exposes the athlete to front lever, back lever, muscle-up, handstand and human-flag practice. A workout number never proves readiness for a harder variation.

## Findings addressed

The intended audience is recreational athletes building from fundamentals through intermediate strength, with the complete advanced skill ladders retained. Foundations effort is capped at RPE 6, Levels 2-4 at RPE 7, and later authored sessions at RPE 8. Planche, straddle and full-lever variations remain in the later progression as short practice ceilings, with easier regressions. The most advanced full-planche/full-lever exposures use at most three short sets; completing a level never requires mastery of those shapes. Early standalone muscle-ups use assistance, lever raises use tuck holds, and windshield-wiper work uses knee raises. Progress follows control and tolerance, with no deadline.


- Ninety source workouts had generic titles. Every effective class now has a descriptive name.
- Twenty early strength sessions exceeded 20 sets; the largest had 45. The working projection caps total main/practice sets at 20, retains every exercise, limits per-exercise work and replaces unlimited max prescriptions with conservative ceilings. Twenty-four early slots are now recovery mornings.
- Handstand and human-flag development were missing. Nine movement definitions and matching demonstrations add supported, prerequisite-based ladders to a static shared timetable.
- Session readiness/safety fields were empty and ignored by the parser. They are populated and available from a concise coaching card on overview and active screens.
- Rows and assisted pulls lost floor/box contact, some elbow solutions flipped between frames, and dragon-flag/candlestick torso directions were reversed. Contact-aware poses and sampled checks correct these errors. Push-ups, assisted dips, low-bar transitions, side planks and straight-leg raises also received corrections. Isometric lever and planche guides now hold the named shape.
- Exercise guides could not be paused or slowed, and compound sets showed one blended interpretation. Controls now support pause, half-speed playback and individual named-variation previews. Tempo codes are expanded into readable instructions.
- Rest disappeared after the last set of each exercise. The app now offers the prescribed rest before the next exercise too, ending it only after the final workout set.

## Verification

The existing full-curriculum and animation validators remain required. A new morning-class check covers all 600 sessions, both new skill ladders, prior rehearsal of their regressions, recovery replacement, early workload limits, and hand/foot contacts sampled between keyframes. Android compilation and lint, full-library contact-sheet inspection, and emulator interaction checks complement the data tests. Packaging runs the new check as well.

## Limits relevant to a future paid product

These are simplified two-dimensional movement cues. Side-view drawings cannot fully communicate grip orientation, three-dimensional rotation, shoulder positioning or spotting, particularly for windshield wipers, hip rotation, straddles and advanced bar transitions. Written instructions remain essential. The catalog does not assess technique, detect pain or automatically establish skill mastery. Full human flags are not prescribed as a guaranteed final milestone.

Before selling this as personal coaching, arrange an in-person movement review by a qualified calisthenics coach and usability testing with beginners and experienced trainees, including supported alternatives and safe exits. This revision improves the working product; it is not a claim of clinical validation or commercial readiness.
